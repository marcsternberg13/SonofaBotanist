My core interest

Cultural vanguardism

Societies change when someone leads them toward ideas, people and beliefs they did not yet accept. I study those society-level change leaders: artists and storytellers who move culture through the work they make, and the institutions, from awards and museums to schools and employers, that decide whose work is seen and who gets in.

I believe schools and stories do the same work. Both change what people think is possible and which beliefs they are allowed to question. That is why I want to do this research in a school of education.

My larger question is whether cultural production can signal institutional change before institutions catch up.

The research

Four streams, one question

Each stream tests the same question in a different setting, and three are tied to a Son of a Botanist Productions film.

Film and its gatekeepers

What Do the Rewarded Want?

What motivates Oscar-nominated directors and actors, and do they see themselves as cultural vanguards?

MSc thesis, Oxford and HEC Paris. 132 published interviews with 16 nominees from the 97th and 98th Academy Awards.
Due April 2027
Why it matters: it tests whether the people institutions reward know they are shaping culture.
Women in the arts

Consent as Conferral

How do under-recognized women artists, and the gatekeepers around them, respond when an outside institution offers them significance?

Cowgirls: Women of Western Art. 58 documentary interviews treated as data.
Preliminary coding complete
Early finding: 90% signed. Every negotiation came from an artist; every refusal and stall came from an institution.
The Trail of Tears and Indigenous art

The Encoding Trap

Why does Poteet Victory's abstract work circulate while his commissioned Trail of Tears paintings have never been shown, and does reading them depend on what viewers were taught?

Trail with No End and Outside the Frame. A fixed eight-question viewer study.
Four pilot sessions complete
Why it matters: if recognition depends on schooling, curriculum decides who can read the work.
AI and bottom-rung workers

The Stolen Ladder

Fields have always trained newcomers through entry-level work. What happens to training, and to who gets in, when AI absorbs that work?

Interview study across levels of AI exposure and career stage.
Designed
Why it matters: when the lowest rung disappears, so does the way newcomers learn a field.
